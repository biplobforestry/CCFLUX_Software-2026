Acquisition quality

    MicaSense is read for its acquisition metadata only. Radiometry, reflectance and vegetation indices are done by the standalone MicaSense pipeline, not here.

    Camera and light sensor

      Read from the XMP of the band this review decodes for each capture. Nothing here is calibrated or corrected.

      Capture integrity

      Every trigger by image number and what it delivered. A capture short of six bands cannot be band-aligned downstream; one with no usable acquisition time cannot be placed on the flight at all.

      Capture cadence

      Seconds per trigger from the camera's boot clock, divided by the image-number gap, as the standalone MicaSense QA computes it. Dividing by the gap makes a break in the numbering read as one slow interval rather than a spike, and the boot clock keeps working when the GPS never locks. The dashed line is 1.5× the expected interval.

      Flight track

      Capture positions from image GPS, coloured by GPS altitude. Metadata positions only — no photogrammetry is performed.

      Altitude

      GPS altitude beside the camera's barometric altitude. A steady offset between them is normal; divergence indicates one of the two drifting.

      Downwelling irradiance

      The DLS record of incoming light at each trigger. This is what a later reflectance conversion would divide by, so its stability bounds what that conversion can achieve.

      Exposure and gain

      Exposure time and ISO per capture. Both moving together with irradiance is auto-exposure working; either pinned at a limit means the scene was outside the camera's range.

      Light sensor orientation

      DLS yaw, pitch and roll in the radians the sensor writes, unconverted, so these match the standalone MicaSense overview CSV value for value. The irradiance reading assumes a level sensor, so large pitch or roll excursions are where it is least trustworthy.

      Solar geometry

      Solar elevation and azimuth exactly as the DLS recorded them, in the radians it writes and unconverted, so these match the standalone MicaSense overview CSV value for value. They should track the sun across the flight; a flat line means the sensor stopped recomputing them.

      Imager temperature

      Sensor body temperature per capture. Dark current rises with it, which matters for any later radiometric work.

      Panchromatic image quality

      Sharpness of every tenth capture's panchromatic band: mean squared gradient normalised by the 1–99 percentile spread, so captures of different brightness are comparable. Sampled, because that band is 10 MB and a flight holds thousands. The dashed line is the bottom 5% of this flight — points below it are its softest frames, not an absolute blur limit.

      Band completeness

      How many of the six bands each trigger delivered. A capture short of six bands cannot be band-aligned downstream.

      Sample images

      One band per sampled capture, contrast-stretched between its 2nd and 98th percentile so the 16-bit scene is visible. A display stretch only: the stored counts are untouched.
